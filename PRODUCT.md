# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Fashion shoppers browsing on their phones — often one-handed, on the go, in short sessions. They discover dresses, compare silhouettes, save favorites, purchase, and track orders. A secondary admin persona manages the boutique catalog, inventory, and orders from the same app.

## Product Purpose

A premium mobile boutique for dresses ("LUNE"): discover, evaluate, purchase, and track dresses through a simple, beautiful interface. Success is a shopping experience that feels like a real fashion boutique — editorial, calm, trustworthy — rather than a generic shopping catalog.

## Brand Personality

Premium, editorial, minimal. Boutique voice in copy ("Your boutique awaits"), warm paper-and-ink palette with a single clay accent, Newsreader serif as the typographic signature. Confidence through restraint, not decoration.

## Anti-references

- Generic e-commerce dashboards: uniform bordered cards, rows of identical icon tiles.
- Template CRUD look: excessive cards, shadows, borders, gradients.
- Loud marketplace energy: banners, badges, urgency colors, clutter.
- AI-default tells: gradient text, side-stripe borders, tiny tracked uppercase on every section.

## Design Principles

1. Typography is the interface — Newsreader serif carries moments of identity; a restrained sans handles everything functional.
2. One accent (clay) carries emotion; everything else is ink on paper.
3. Structure over chrome — hairlines and spacing group content; cards are the exception, not the default.
4. Motion is material ("The Atelier") — line-art moments, quiet ease-outs, everything honors reduce-motion.
5. Every state exists — loading, empty, error, and success are designed, never blank.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA contrast targets for body text (≥4.5:1).
- Reduce Motion honored everywhere (Reanimated ReduceMotion.System, Lottie reduce-motion switch).
- Touch targets ≥44pt effective (hitSlop where visuals are smaller).
- Accessibility labels on all icon-only controls.
- Client-side checks are never security boundaries; authorization lives in RLS.
